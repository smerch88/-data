SaaS Churn Rate Benchmarks: What 500+ Companies Report in 2026

SaaS churn is a major challenge, costing companies billions annually. In 2026, the median monthly churn rate for B2B SaaS is 3.5%, with 2.6% from voluntary cancellations and 0.8%-0.9% from billing issues. Successful companies focus on Net Revenue Retention (NRR), with top performers exceeding 120%, driving growth without constant new customer acquisition.

Key highlights:

Churn by company size: Early-stage startups (<$1M ARR) face 5%-7% monthly churn, while enterprise SaaS with large contracts enjoys rates as low as 0.5%-1%.
Industry differences: Infrastructure SaaS has the lowest churn (1.8%), while EdTech struggles with the highest (9.6%).
Billing matters: Involuntary churn accounts for 20%-40% of all churn but is often preventable.
Retention priorities: Simplify onboarding, use AI to predict churn, and shift to annual billing or usage-based pricing to reduce attrition.

Retention drives valuations. Companies with NRR above 130% trade at 15-20× forward revenue, while those below 100% struggle with just 3-5×. Focus on the first 90 days, fix billing issues, and refine pricing to improve retention and long-term growth.

2026 SaaS Churn Rate Benchmarks by Company Stage and Industry

Average Churn Rates Across All SaaS Companies

In 2026, the median monthly churn rate for B2B SaaS companies stands at 3.5%. Breaking it down, 2.6% is voluntary churn, while 0.8%–0.9% stems from involuntary churn caused by billing issues. Interestingly, about a quarter of churn cases are tied to billing errors, which are often preventable.

"Involuntary churn is the silent killer of SaaS growth... perfectly happy customers are slipping through your fingers because of fixable billing errors." - FeatureBot 

When it comes to Net Revenue Retention (NRR), median rates for leading B2B SaaS companies range between 101% and 114%, with top performers exceeding 120%. Companies achieving NRR above 130% often see valuations of 15–20× forward revenue. In contrast, those with NRR below 100% typically trade at just 3–5×.

Let’s take a closer look at how company size and stage influence these benchmarks.

Churn Rates by Company Stage and Size

Churn rates vary significantly depending on a company's stage and size. Early-stage companies (under $1 million ARR) face the highest monthly churn at 5% to 7%. Meanwhile, scale-stage companies (over $15 million ARR) tend to have churn rates between 2% and 3%. Enterprise SaaS, which deals with contracts over $100,000 ACV, enjoys the lowest churn, ranging from 0.5% to 1% monthly.

Interestingly, software endorsed by C-suite executives tends to churn 3.6× slower than tools purchased by individual contributors or managers. When a CEO backs a platform, it often becomes deeply embedded in the company’s operations.

"Champion turnover is the #1 killer. Your internal champion gets promoted, leaves, or gets reorged out... the renewal is in 90 days." - Prospeo Team 

Churn Rates by Industry and Pricing Model

Churn rates also depend heavily on the industry and pricing models. For example, Infrastructure SaaS sees the lowest monthly churn at 1.8%, while EdTech struggles with the highest churn at 9.6%, a rate that has doubled since 2024. Similarly, Healthcare SaaS experienced a 67% spike in revenue churn between 2024 and 2025.

Pricing models play a big role too. Annual prepaid contracts typically see churn rates between 0.5% and 2% monthly, whereas month-to-month subscriptions face churn rates ranging from 3% to 8%. To combat this, many companies are shifting to annual billing with discounts of 15–20%, reducing the risk of monthly re-evaluations.

Emerging tech segments, like AI-native SaaS, present unique retention challenges. Their median gross revenue retention sits at 40%, with budget-tier AI products under $50/month retaining only 23% of users. However, the "AI tourist" effect, where users sign up out of curiosity rather than need, has eased. Gross revenue retention climbed from 27% in January 2025 to 40% by September 2025. This highlights the importance of tailored retention strategies for newer tech categories.

Alongside these tech advancements, shifts in pricing strategies are also helping to improve retention rates.

Pricing Changes That Improve Retention

Switching to annual billing has proven to be a game-changer for reducing churn. Annual subscribers churn at about one-third the rate of monthly subscribers, with churn rates dropping by as much as 60%. Many SaaS companies now default to annual contracts, sweetening the deal with 15%–20% discounts to discourage monthly cancellations.

Another effective strategy is usage-based and per-seat pricing, which naturally encourages revenue growth. By 2026, 40% of SaaS companies with $15 million to $30 million in ARR have achieved "negative churn" through these expansion-friendly pricing models. Median Net Revenue Retention (NRR) for SaaS companies now ranges from 106% to 110%, while top-tier companies are hitting NRR levels of 120%–130% through these pricing tactics.

Pricing tiers also play a significant role in retention. Budget-tier AI products priced under $50 per month only retain 23% of users, while premium tiers costing over $250 per month see a much stronger 70% gross revenue retention. This disparity has driven AI-focused companies to move away from low-cost offerings and toward premium pricing models that promote stability.

Retention has become a central focus for SaaS businesses, and for good reason. Investors now care more about capital efficiency and high Net Revenue Retention than aggressive customer acquisition. NRR has emerged as the top indicator of a company's valuation. For instance, companies with NRR above 130% are trading at 15–20× forward revenue, while those below 100% struggle with multiples of just 3–5×.

Success now depends on creating long-term customer value and ensuring product stickiness rather than relying on flashy acquisition tactics. Churn remains a massive issue, with failed payments alone accounting for 20%–40% of all churn - a problem that costs the SaaS industry a staggering $129 billion annually.

How to Reduce Your SaaS Churn Rate

Here are some actionable strategies to help you lower churn rates, drawing insights from trends shaping retention in 2026.

Simplify Your Onboarding Process

Helping users reach their "aha moment" within 7 days can drastically reduce churn. Why? Companies that achieve this see 50% lower churn rates compared to those with longer onboarding times. This is especially important since 60% to 70% of churn happens during the first 90 days of a customer’s journey.

To improve retention, focus on removing any barriers between signup and the first major milestone (like inviting a team member, generating a report, or uploading a file). Inefficient onboarding is responsible for 23% of B2B SaaS churn. By creating dedicated "onboarding success" roles - separate from general customer success teams - companies have reduced churn in the first 90 days by 30% to 40%.

"If 65% of churn happens in the first 90 days, 65% of your retention investment should be concentrated there." – Bruno Ueda, Co-Founder, PipelineRoad 

A simple framework can make all the difference: launch in-app guided tutorials on Day 1, follow up with personal outreach by Day 7 if no key action is completed, and schedule a usage-focused check-in by Day 30. This structured approach ensures users stay engaged during the critical early days.

Use Email, Push, and In-App Messages to Re-Engage Users

Customer usage typically drops by 41% in the quarter leading up to cancellation. This gives you a crucial 3- to 6-week window to re-engage them. A multi-channel strategy - combining email, push notifications, and in-app messages - can work wonders when triggered by user behavior.

Start by segmenting users into groups like "Power Users", "Drifting", and "At-Risk". For instance, if a user signs up but doesn’t complete a core task within two hours, send an automated email. For users logging in less frequently, consider weekly "value" emails that highlight their progress (e.g., "You tracked 1,247 events this week") to remind them of the product’s benefits.

For failed payments, hybrid dunning can help. This approach combines smart payment retries with email and SMS sequences, recovering 80% to 90% of failed payments within 30 days. Here’s a proven timeline: send a friendly automated email on Day 0, a follow-up on Day 3 with more urgency, personal outreach by Day 7, and a final multi-channel warning on Day 14.

Test Different Pricing Models

Adjusting your pricing strategy can also reduce churn by aligning costs with perceived value. For example, usage-based or annual billing models have been shown to lower churn significantly.

Usage-based pricing lowers attrition rates by 46% compared to per-seat models, reducing monthly churn from 3.9% to 2.1%. This approach works because customers feel they’re paying for what they use, avoiding the feeling of being overcharged. It also supports growth: usage-based models generate 47% annual expansion revenue, compared to just 18% for per-seat models.

If your product is priced in the $25–$50 range (which has the highest revenue churn at 8.6%), consider repositioning it into a higher-priced, more mission-critical tier. Products in higher price brackets are often deeply embedded into workflows and involve more stakeholders, making them harder to cancel.

FAQs
What churn rate should I target for my SaaS stage and ACV?

For early-stage SaaS companies with a lower average contract value (ACV), a monthly churn rate between 3-5% is a reasonable target. On the other hand, enterprise-level SaaS businesses should aim for a churn rate of less than 1.5% per month. These figures aren't set in stone - they can shift depending on your company's stage and the type of customers you serve. Make sure to adapt these benchmarks to fit your unique business objectives.

How do I separate voluntary churn from billing (involuntary) churn?

To distinguish between voluntary and involuntary churn, start by examining why customers leave and reviewing payment failure data.

Voluntary churn happens when customers actively decide to leave, often due to dissatisfaction with the service or opting for a competitor.
Involuntary churn, on the other hand, occurs because of payment-related issues, like expired credit cards or failed transactions.

Using automated payment recovery tools can help address involuntary churn by resolving payment issues promptly. This not only reduces customer loss but also makes it easier to categorize churn types, allowing you to focus on more effective retention strategies.

Which 90-day retention actions move NRR the fastest?

To increase NRR in just 90 days, prioritize a few critical areas: streamlining the onboarding process, driving product engagement, quickly addressing support issues, and applying targeted retention strategies. These steps tackle common reasons for churn while fostering opportunities for customers to grow their usage and investment in your product.
